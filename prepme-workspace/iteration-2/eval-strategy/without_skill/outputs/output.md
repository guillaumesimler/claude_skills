## Strategy Class Prep — Porter's Five Forces / Airlines

Porter's Five Forces is a framework for analyzing industry structure. Applied to airlines, it shows why the sector earns chronically low returns:

**Rivalry** is extreme — undifferentiated product, price-transparent aggregators, high fixed costs, recurrent price wars.

**Supplier power** is high — Boeing/Airbus duopoly, unionized pilots in shortage, commodity fuel.

**Buyer power** is high — zero switching costs, price transparency, corporate negotiation power.

**New entrants** remain a moderate threat despite capital barriers — LCCs have proven entry is possible.

**Substitutes** are low to moderate — rail on short-haul, video for business travel.

Conclusion: all five forces are simultaneously unfavorable, explaining chronic low profitability.

### Things to think about before class

- The US consolidation wave (2008–2014) restructured the competitive landscape — does Five Forces account for this?
- Delta's ROIC beat the cost of capital for several years in the same structurally unattractive industry — what does that say about the framework?
- Porter's framework is static; real strategy involves reshaping industry structure, not just reacting to it.

### Recent context

Airlines are pushing into premium cabins and AI pricing in 2026 as a strategic response to commodity rivalry. Aircraft delivery shortfalls (5,300 jets behind schedule) continue to constrain fleet strategy.
