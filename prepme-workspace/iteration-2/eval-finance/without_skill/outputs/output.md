## Corporate Finance Prep — WACC & Capital Structure

### Key concepts to know

**MM Proposition I:** Firm value is independent of capital structure in perfect markets — investors can replicate any leverage themselves.

**MM Proposition II:** Cost of equity rises with leverage, offsetting cheaper debt. WACC stays flat without taxes.

**With taxes:** Debt creates a tax shield — `V_L = V_U + T_c × D`. WACC falls as leverage rises.

**Tradeoff theory:** Optimal leverage balances tax shield against financial distress costs. Asset-heavy, stable-cashflow firms (utilities) lever up; high-growth intangible firms (tech) stay light.

**WACC formula:** `(E/V)r_E + (D/V)r_D(1-T_c)`

### Tesla case

Tesla moved from near-bankruptcy (2019) to near-zero debt (2021) by issuing equity when its stock was at all-time highs, preserving growth option value. By 2023 it began returning to conventional leverage as profitability stabilized.

### Things to think about before class

- If the tax shield is so valuable, why do profitable firms systematically under-leverage? (The "leverage puzzle")
- Pecking order theory: firms prefer internal funds → debt → equity. Does Tesla's equity issuance contradict this?
- Market timing (Baker & Wurgler): capital structure reflects opportunistic financing, not optimization toward a target.

### Common cold-call questions

- Why doesn't every firm maximize debt? Financial distress costs rise non-linearly; growth options are destroyed.
- What did MM actually prove? That every real-world deviation from their assumptions (taxes, distress, info asymmetry) matters.
