## Macroeconomics Prep — Monetary Policy & the Fed

### Core concepts

**Taylor Rule:** `i = r* + π + 0.5(π - π*) + 0.5(y - y*)` — prescribes higher rates when inflation exceeds target and/or when output exceeds potential. Named after economist John Taylor (1993).

**The 2022–2024 cycle:** Fed hiked 525 bps in 16 months — fastest since Volcker. Peak rate: 5.25–5.50% (July 2023). Cuts began September 2024. By end-2024: 4.25–4.50%.

**Current status (April 2026):** Fed holding at 3.5–3.75%. Next FOMC meeting April 28–29. Markets pricing in no cuts for 2026 — tariff-driven inflation risk keeping the Fed on hold.

**Transmission mechanism:** FFR rise → higher borrowing costs → lower spending → lower demand → lower inflation. Lag: 12–24 months.

**Key debates:**
- Was the soft landing skill or luck? (Supply chains healed independently)
- Has r* (neutral rate) shifted higher? If so, 3.5% may not be restrictive
- Tariff shock = supply-side inflation the Taylor Rule wasn't built for

### Things to think about before class

- The Taylor Rule prescribed 5%+ in mid-2021. The Fed held near zero. Who was right and why?
- SVB failed because of rapid rate hikes — did the Fed create financial instability while fighting inflation?
- Stagflation risk in 2026: rate hikes can't fix supply-side price pressure, only demand destruction.
